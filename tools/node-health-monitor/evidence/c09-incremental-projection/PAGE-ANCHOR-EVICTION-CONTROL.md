`page_eviction_keeps_last_process_anchor_and_reopen_refuses_tamper` inserts 16 distinct real M process rows, reads one bounded global-M page, and imports it into a disposable Q `EvidenceStore` capped at **8,000 bytes**. It requires actual eviction of older projected rows, retention of the page's last process original in `query_origins`, and successful cursor commit **without** a non-process boundary witness. It then reopens the Q ledger, SQL-tampers the cursor to a syntactically valid false anchor, reopens again, and requires both the source-bound M reader and QueryService startup to refuse the changed anchor.

Why the last process anchor cannot be evicted *by this same successful page*: `EvidenceStore::insert` charges each individual serialized record plus 2,048 bytes, refuses a record larger than its cap, evicts only from the existing front **before** appending the new record, and never removes the just-appended newest record. `insert_projection_page` visits at most 256 M rows in order. Every new M process row has a distinct immutable M ID in its projected `source_record_id`, so a successful page's last process record is its last Q insertion. Later diagnostic/global rows in the page do not insert Q evidence; they instead supply a separately witnessed global boundary. The production Q cap remains 8 MiB; the 8,000-byte test cap forces the relevant eviction more aggressively without raising a limit.
